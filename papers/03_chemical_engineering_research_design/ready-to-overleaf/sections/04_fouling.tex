\section{Reaction-driven fouling and time to runaway}
\label{sec:fouling}

\subsection{Fouling kinetics}
Fouling of reactor jackets by deposits that form from the reacting medium, e.g.\ polymer films, is commonly described by a deposition rate proportional to the reaction rate and an asymptotic fouling resistance \cite{bott1995fouling,epstein1983thinking,macchietto2011fouling}. We adopt the simplest law with these two features,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d(UA)}{dt} = -\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}}\; r_A(C_A,T)\;\bigl(UA-UA_{\min}\bigr),
\label{eq:fouling}
\end{equation}
with $UA(0)=UA_0$, and append it to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mass}--\eqref{eq:jacket} as a fourth state. Because $r_A\ge0$, the right-hand side is non-positive whenever $UA\ge UA_{\min}$ and vanishes at $UA=UA_{\min}$; by uniqueness of solutions, $UA(t)$ is non-increasing and never crosses $UA_{\min}$. The coupling with the reaction rate makes fouling faster at hot, high-rate operating points. The values $\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}}=\kappaFoul$~L/mol and $UA_{\min}=\uaMin\%\,UA_0$ are scenario parameters: $\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}}$ compresses into tens of minutes a degradation that in practice takes days or weeks, so the results below are reported against the prediction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_pred}, whose time scale is $1/(\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}}r_A)$, rather than as absolute times. $UA_{\min}$ is chosen below the critical value of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ua_crit} at both operating points, so that the valve must eventually saturate.

The four-state system is integrated between control samples with an adaptive Runge--Kutta method (relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-10}$) with the coolant flow held constant over each interval. The monotonicity and the lower bound of $UA(t)$ are verified by property-based tests over random $(\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}},UA_{\min})$ pairs.

\subsection{Quasi-steady time to valve saturation}
While the controller holds $T$ and $C_A$ at their setpoints, $r_A$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fouling} is constant and the fouling law integrates in closed form, $UA(t)=UA_{\min}+(UA_0-UA_{\min})\,e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}}\bar r_At}$. The valve must reach $u_{\max}$ when $UA(t)=UA_{\mathrm{crit}}$, i.e.\ at
\begin{equation}
t_{\mathrm{pred}} = \frac{1}{\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}}\,\bar r_A}\,\ln\frac{UA_0-UA_{\min}}{UA_{\mathrm{crit}}-UA_{\min}},
\label{eq:t_pred}
\end{equation}
and the temperature leaves the setpoint after that. With $\bar r_A=\rANom$ and $\rASad$~mol/(L\,min) at the nominal point and at the saddle, Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_pred} gives $t_{\mathrm{pred}}=\tpredNom$ and $\tpredSad$~min respectively. Equation~\eqref{eq:t_pred} assumes that the loop tracks the slow drift of the plant; whether it does is exactly what the simulations test. We report the first time the valve reaches $u_{\max}$, $t_{\mathrm{sat}}$, and the first time the temperature exceeds the setpoint by more than $\bandRun$~K, $t_{\mathrm{runaway}}$.

\subsection{Frozen-fouling stability of fixed-gain loops}
\label{subsec:frozen}
Fouling changes the plant seen by the controller. For a frozen value of $UA$, the equilibrium at the same setpoint has a lower jacket temperature, $\bar T_j=\bar T-Q_{\mathrm{load}}/UA$, and therefore, by Eq.~\eqref{eq:jacobian}, a smaller actuator gain $|b_3|=(\bar T_j-T_{jf})/V_j$. At the critical capacity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ua_crit} the jacket sits at $\bar T_j=\TjCrit$~K, so $b_3=(T_{jf}-\bar T_j)/V_j=\bThreeCrit$~K/L, against $\bThreeNom$~K/L at $UA_0$: a factor of $\bThreeRatio$. (The script evaluates $b_3$ at $UA_{\mathrm{crit}}$ itself and checks it against the Jacobian just above it; an earlier evaluation at a grid point $1\%$ above $UA_{\mathrm{crit}}$ gave a smaller factor.)

To see what this does to a controller tuned at $UA_0$, the saddle equilibrium is recomputed for frozen values of $UA$ between $UA_0$ and $UA_{\mathrm{crit}}$, and the closed-loop spectrum is evaluated with the gains of Section~\ref{sec:design}: the SIMC single loop, the optimized single loop, the cascade, and the cascade with the scheduled slave gain of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gs_slave}. The value of $UA$ at which the spectral abscissa crosses zero, found by Brent's method, is the \emph{analytical linear stability boundary} of that loop. It does not depend on $\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}}$, because the linearization is frozen: it is the boundary that an infinitely slow fouling would meet. How a finite rate departs from it is the subject of Section~\ref{subsec:res_sweep}.

\subsection{Mechanism: a Hopf bifurcation, not a loss of static gain}
\label{subsec:mechanism}
A smaller $|b_3|$ lowers the static loop gain, and for the unstable pole of this reactor the static condition is sharp: under proportional action $K_ck>1$ is necessary and sufficient (Section~\ref{subsec:siso_design}). It would be natural to read the loss of stability under fouling as that condition failing. The frozen-$UA$ spectra show that it does not. Let $k(UA)=-G(0)$ of the full third-order model at each frozen equilibrium. For the SIMC gain, $K_ck$ falls from $\KcKnom$ at $UA_0$ to $\KcKSiso$ at the single-loop boundary ($\fstabSiso\%$) and to $\KcKCascade$ at the cascade boundary ($\fstabCascade\%$); both exceed the static threshold of $1$. At both boundaries the script finds the dominant closed-loop eigenvalues on the imaginary axis as a complex-conjugate pair, $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda=0$ and $\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda=\pm\wHopfSiso$ (single loop) and $\pm\wHopfCascade$~rad/min (cascade), i.e.\ periods of $\perHopfSiso$ and $\perHopfCascade$~min, and raises an error if the crossing is a real pole at the origin instead. The loss of stability is an oscillatory (Hopf) bifurcation of a loop whose static gain is adequate.

The two architectures reach the bifurcation from opposite sides.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{SIMC single loop.} The loop is conditionally stable with a lower gain-margin factor of only $\gmDownSimc$ (Table~\ref{tab:margins}), because its phase margin is $\pmSimc^\circ$: the gain may be lowered by that factor and no more. The fall of $k(UA)$ from $UA_0$ to $\fstabSiso\%$ is $\KcKSiso/\KcKnom$, which is that margin ($\KcKnom\times\gmDownSimc\approx\KcKSiso$). The declining actuator gain is therefore the parameter that drives the pair across, but the threshold, $K_ck\approx1.5$, is fixed by the phase margin of the loop, not by the static condition at $1$.
    \item \emph{Cascade.} With the slave loop closed the master loop sees the static gain $K_1$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:k1_exact}, which \emph{grows} as fouling pushes $\det\mathbf{A}_{12}$ towards zero. The master static gain $K_{c1}K_1$ rises from $\KcOneKOneNom$ at $UA_0$ to $\KcOneKOneCascade$ at $\fstabCascade\%$ ($K_1$ diverges where $\det\mathbf{A}_{12}$ vanishes). The cascade is lost by too much master gain; $b_3$ plays no role in it.
\end{itemize}
Neither case is a collapse of the static gain, and the second is not even a loss of gain. Figure~\ref{fig:frozen} shows $K_ck$ and $K_{c1}K_1$ against $UA$, the trajectory of the dominant pair and the spectral abscissa.
