\section{Results and discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Regulation of the saddle under utility and feed shocks}
\label{subsec:res_shocks}
Five controllers of Section~\ref{sec:design} start at the saddle and face either a step of $+10$~K in the coolant supply temperature or a step of $+5$~K in the feed temperature, applied at $t=\tShock$~min and held until the end of the fixed $\horizonShockWin$~min window. Besides the SIMC single loop, the cascade and the cascade with feedforward, the table includes the $M_s$-constrained single loop of Section~\ref{subsec:siso_opt} and the cascade with the scheduled slave gain. Table~\ref{tab:saddle_shocks} lists the integral absolute error (IAE), the peak deviation, the total variation (TV) of the valve, the largest coolant flow and the peak-to-peak amplitude of $T$ over the last $\ptpWin$~min, which separates a settled response from a sustained oscillation. Figure~\ref{fig:shocks} shows the trajectories.

\paragraph{Coolant supply shock.} The SIMC single loop does not return to the setpoint. It enters a sustained oscillation with a peak deviation of $\peakTjfSiso$~K and a peak-to-peak amplitude of $\ptpTjfSiso$~K at the end of the window. This is not a slow transient. At the post-shock equilibrium ($T_{jf}=310$~K, $q_j=\qjShockTjf$~L/min) the jacket is closer to the supply temperature, the actuator gain drops to $b_3=\bThreeShockTjf$~K/L, and the linearized SIMC loop has spectral abscissa $+\absShockTjfSiso$~min$^{-1}$: the shock moved the reactor to a point that these gains no longer stabilize, and the bounded oscillation is the limit cycle of the nonlinear loop around an unstable closed-loop equilibrium. The same disturbance leaves the optimized single loop at $\absShockTjfSisoopt$~min$^{-1}$ and the cascade at $\absShockTjfCascade$~min$^{-1}$.

Measured against the SIMC loop, the cascade reduces the IAE by $\iaeRedSimcTjf\%$. That number is not a measure of cascade control: the SIMC loop has $M_s=\msSimc$. Against the optimized single loop, which settles to $\ptpTjfSisoopt$~K peak to peak with an IAE of $\iaeTjfSisoopt$~K\,min and a peak deviation of $\peakTjfSisoopt$~K, the cascade has an IAE of $\iaeTjfCascade$~K\,min, a smaller peak ($\peakTjfCascade$~K) and a larger valve travel; the fair IAE change of the cascade is $\iaeRedTjf\%$ (negative: worse). Here the classical argument for cascade control \cite{shinskey1996process,seborg2010process,astrom2006pid}, that the slave loop removes utility disturbances before they reach the reactor, holds as a smaller peak, not as a smaller integrated error, because the master loop uses the default SIMC setting and is slow to return. Feedforward from $T_{jf}$ through Eq.~\eqref{eq:ff} brings the cascade to $\iaeTjfCascadeff$~K\,min and the peak to $\peakTjfCascadeff$~K, the only configuration that beats the optimized single loop on this shock. The scheduled slave gain changes this shock negligibly ($\iaeTjfCascadegs$~K\,min), because $T_j$ stays within a few kelvin of its clean value.

\paragraph{Feed temperature shock.} The feed shock enters the reactor energy balance directly, upstream of anything the slave can measure. The optimized single loop absorbs it with a peak deviation of $\peakTfSisoopt$~K and an IAE of $\iaeTfSisoopt$~K\,min. The cascade does not settle within the window ($\ptpTfCascade$~K peak to peak over the last $\ptpWin$~min, peak $\peakTfCascade$~K, IAE $\iaeTfCascade$~K\,min), although its linearization at the post-shock equilibrium is stable ($\absShockTfCascade$~min$^{-1}$): the master design $\tau_{c1}=\theta_1$ is the default SIMC choice, not a tightened one, and the master integral time is short compared with the reactor response, so a large disturbance reaches the valve limits (largest flow $\qmaxTfCascade$~L/min). The scheduled slave gain gives an IAE of $\iaeTfCascadegs$~K\,min. As expected, the feedforward of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ff} does nothing here, and the two cascade rows coincide.

The shocks therefore show that the benefit usually attributed to cascade control at an unstable operating point---a stable plant for the master (Section~\ref{subsec:cascade_design})---is real, but it does not translate into better regulation than a well-tuned single loop unless the master loop is also tuned. The cascade master was not optimized in this work, so the comparison with the optimized single loop favours the latter.

\subsection{Continuous fouling}
\label{subsec:res_fouling}
Table~\ref{tab:fouling} and Fig.~\ref{fig:fouling} report the fouling runs at $\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}}=\kappaFoul$~L/mol. The two operating points behave in qualitatively different ways.

\paragraph{Nominal point: the thermodynamic limit is the binding one.} Both controllers hold the temperature while the valve opens, and the valve reaches $u_{\max}$ at $t_{\mathrm{sat}}=\tsatNomCascade$~min (cascade) and $\tsatNomSiso$~min (single loop), against $t_{\mathrm{pred}}=\tpredNom$~min from Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_pred}. The heat-transfer capacity at saturation, $\uaSatNomCascade\%$ and $\uaSatNomSiso\%$ of $UA_0$, is just below the $\fcritNom\%$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ua_crit}; the small delay is the tracking lag of each loop, larger for the slower single loop. After saturation the temperature leaves the setpoint ($t_{\mathrm{runaway}}=\trunNomCascade$~min for both) and climbs smoothly to $\TendNomCascade$~K, the stable equilibrium of the fouled reactor with the valve fully open. Because the nominal heat balance has a single root, there is no jump: the loss of regulation is a drift along the only branch, and its onset is predicted by a steady-state calculation.

\paragraph{Saddle: the loops fail long before the cooling capacity does, and the failure is controller-specific.} At the saddle the quasi-steady prediction is $t_{\mathrm{pred}}=\tpredSad$~min, but the SIMC loops do not get there. The SIMC single loop first exceeds the $\bandRun$~K band at $\trunSadSiso$~min, when the capacity has fallen to $\uaRunPctSadSiso\%$ of $UA_0$---the \emph{practical loss of regulation} of this loop at this rate---and the cascade at $\trunSadCascade$~min ($\uaRunPctSadCascade\%$). The optimized single loop holds until $\trunSadSisoopt$~min ($\uaRunPctSadSisoopt\%$); the scheduled cascade is indistinguishable from the plain cascade. The cascade leaves the band at a capacity \emph{above} its own frozen boundary ($\fstabCascade\%$), so at this fouling rate its departure cannot be the Hopf crossing; it must be a transient response to the fast decline of $UA$. In every case the valve then saturates during an excursion and the reactor ignites to the hot branch, ending near $\TendSadCascade$~K. The capacity at saturation, between $\uaSatSadCascade\%$ and $\uaSatSadSisoopt\%$, is still above the critical $\fcritSad\%$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ua_crit}: the valve saturates during the excursion, not because the heat balance required more cooling than it could deliver.

\subsection{Fouling rate and the frozen-$UA$ boundary}
\label{subsec:res_sweep}
The frozen-$UA$ boundaries of Section~\ref{subsec:mechanism} ($\fstabCascade$--$\fstabSiso\%$ for the SIMC loops) are not the same thing as the capacity at which a fouling run loses regulation: the former is a property of the linearization at fixed $UA$, the latter of a trajectory in which $UA$ keeps falling while a growing mode needs time to reach $\bandRun$~K. Table~\ref{tab:kappa_sweep} and Fig.~\ref{fig:frozen}(d) vary $\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}}$ over three decades, $\{\kappaSweepLo,\kappaSweepMid,\kappaSweepHi\}$~L/mol, i.e.\ effective rates $\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}}\bar r_A$ of $\rateLo$ to $\rateHi$~min$^{-1}$. Without excitation the noise-free simulation would delay the departure by the integrator's numerical floor rather than by the dynamics, so a deterministic multisine of $\ripple$~K per component at the four Hopf frequencies is added to $T_{jf}$.

\input{tables/cherd_kappa_sweep}

At the slowest rate the departure occurs within $\gapSlowSisoopt$ (optimized single loop), $\gapSlowSiso$ (SIMC single loop), $\gapSlowCascade$ (cascade) and $\gapSlowCascadegs$ (scheduled cascade) percentage points of capacity of the frozen boundary, always \emph{below} it: the loop is already unstable when the departure is detected, and a growing mode needs time to reach $\bandRun$~K. Faster fouling departs at lower capacity for the SIMC loops. The dependence on $\kappa_{\mathrm{foul}}$ is not monotone over the three decades (Table~\ref{tab:kappa_sweep}), because at the fastest rate the departure is set by tracking error rather than by the bifurcation. Three rates do not establish the asymptotic rate of convergence; they show that the linear boundary is the slow-fouling limit and that fast fouling loses regulation well beyond it, at $\uaRunSisoKMid\%$ and $\uaRunSisoKHi\%$ of the clean capacity for the SIMC single loop.

Figure~\ref{fig:frozen} summarizes the mechanism of Section~\ref{subsec:mechanism}: in (a) the static loop gain of the single loop is above $1$ at the crossing, and in (b) the eigenvalue pair reaches the imaginary axis away from the real axis. In (c), the optimized single loop is stable down to $\fstabSisoopt\%$ of the clean capacity, against $\fcritSad\%$ for the thermodynamic limit, while the SIMC loops are lost at $\fstabCascade$--$\fstabSiso\%$. The linear boundary is thus a property of the controller and of its sensitivity, not of the heat exchanger: the controllability margin against fouling on an open-loop unstable branch is $UA_0-UA_{\mathrm{stab}}$, and $UA_{\mathrm{stab}}$ is set by the tuning. Where the equilibrium flows are $\qstabSiso$ and $\qstabCascade$~L/min (SIMC single loop and cascade) the valve is far from its $\umax$~L/min limit.

The gain-scheduling recommendation of Section~\ref{subsec:gs} does not extend the cascade boundary: with the schedule of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gs_slave} the frozen-$UA$ loss moves from $\fstabCascade\%$ to $\fstabCascadegs\%$, the Hopf frequency is unchanged ($\wHopfCascadegs$ against $\wHopfCascade$~rad/min), and the fouling runs of Table~\ref{tab:fouling} and Table~\ref{tab:kappa_sweep} are indistinguishable from the plain cascade. The schedule does what it is designed to do, holding $K_{c2}|b_3|$ constant (Section~\ref{subsec:gs}), but the cascade is not lost in the slave loop: the inner loop stays stable and fast, and the bifurcation comes from the master loop, whose static gain $K_{c1}K_1$ reaches $\KcOneKOneCascade$ at the boundary. Scheduling the master gain on $1/K_1(UA)$ would require an estimate of the heat-transfer capacity; it is not tested here.

\subsection{Parametric sensitivity of the saddle}
\label{subsec:res_mc}
At the saddle the heat balance is hypersensitive to the kinetic parameters. A Sobol sweep of $E/R$, $k_0$ and $\Delta H$ within $\pm\mcSpread\%$ ($\mcN$ points, fixed seed) re-solves, for each point, the coolant flow that holds $T=\TSad$~K; only $\mcFeasible$ of the $\mcN$ points have a saddle at that temperature within the valve range, with required flows from $\mcQjMin$ to $\mcQjMax$~L/min and actuator gains from $\mcBThreeMin$ to $\mcBThreeMax$~K/L. The nominal gains are then applied to the linearization at each perturbed saddle (Table~\ref{tab:montecarlo}). The optimized single loop remains stable at $\mcStableSisoopt\%$ of these points, the SIMC loop at $\mcStableSiso\%$, the cascade at $\mcStableCascade\%$ and the scheduled cascade at $\mcStableCascadegs\%$, so the schedule helps against parametric uncertainty although it does not move the fouling boundary. The sweep is linear and local; it does not test the nonlinear loops.

\subsection{Limitations}
The results rest on one reactor model with lumped, constant physical properties and perfect mixing. The saddle exists only with a feed cooled to $\TfSad$~K, a modification of the benchmark that we introduced because the nominal configuration has no unstable steady state; conclusions about the saddle should not be transferred to the nominal configuration, and the reverse. The fouling law of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fouling} is phenomenological and its rate constant is a scenario parameter chosen to keep the simulations short; the frozen-fouling boundaries are independent of it, and the sweep of Section~\ref{subsec:res_sweep} covers only three rates. Measurements are noise free, the valve has no stiction, and all controllers are PI only; the single loop was optimized and the cascade was not, derivative action and model predictive control were not evaluated on the saddle \cite{rawlings2020model,andrade2026when}, and only the slave gain was scheduled. The stability statements are local, based on the Jacobian, and are confirmed but not proven globally by the nonlinear simulations. The bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ms_bound} is a theorem for the reduced model; its use as a statement about the third-order loop is a heuristic.
