\begin{abstract}
Comparisons of controllers on processes with constrained actuators rarely state the anti-windup logic, although it can decide the verdict. This implementation study examines the integrator logic of PID loops under the amplitude and slew-rate limits of a cooling valve in a reverse-acting loop, the temperature loop of a three-state exothermic CSTR, with six fixed-gain controllers as a test bed. A proposition shows that sign-blind amplitude clamping, a frequent implementation error that reuses the direct-acting freeze condition, latches a reverse-acting loop at full cooling; on the reactor the temperature settles $\vlatchgap$~K below the setpoint and the integral absolute error of a $-2$~K step for Ziegler--Nichols rises from $\vZNdir$ to $\vZNsign$~K$\cdot$min. A seven-policy ablation over three setpoint steps, a load profile, a valve deadband and a second operating point shows that a slew-rate trigger, not sign awareness, restores the loops that demand faster valve moves than the slew limit allows, whereas it worsens the slow SIMC loop; a map over slew limits of $20$ to $320$~L/min$^2$ shows for which loops and valve speeds the trigger matters. Back-calculation with one tracking constant per loop, chosen on the design step, stays within $\vttcvmaxpct\%$ of the directional rule on larger steps, whereas the usual defaults are up to $\vtttimaxpct\%$ worse. The noise-to-valve gain follows the high-frequency controller gain $|K_p|(1+N)$, not the sensitivity peak. A pseudocode and a five-item checklist state what a comparison of controllers on a constrained loop must report.
\end{abstract}
