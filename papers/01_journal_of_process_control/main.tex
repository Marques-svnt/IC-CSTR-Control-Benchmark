\begin{abstract}
Verdicts on PID tuning rules for exothermic reactors depend on implementation details that are rarely reported: the actuator model, the anti-windup logic and the measurement path. This implementation study fixes a three-state nonlinear CSTR whose cooling valve is limited in amplitude and in slew rate and examines how those details change the conclusions drawn for eight PID tunings (Ziegler--Nichols, Cohen--Coon, Tyreus--Luyben, AMIGO and its two-degree-of-freedom variant, Skogestad SIMC, and multi-start ITAE optimization with $M_s \le 1.6$ on the identified dead-time loop, with its two-degree-of-freedom variant). A $2\times 2$ ablation of clamping rules (amplitude-only or amplitude-plus-rate trigger; unconditional or directional freeze), set against no anti-windup and back-calculation, shows that the textbook rule for direct-acting loops latches the valve at its upper stop when the loop is reverse-acting: the reactor settles $16.7$~K below the setpoint and the integral absolute error of a $-2$~K step rises from $0.940$ to $104.123$~K$\cdot$min for Ziegler--Nichols. Rate-aware directional clamping keeps every tuning stable without an extra parameter. On the exact linearization the sensitivity peaks of Ziegler--Nichols and Cohen--Coon are only $1.57$ and $1.75$; their noise vulnerability is set instead by the high-frequency controller gain $|K_p|(1+N)$ ($490$ and $559$~L/min/K against $77$ for SIMC), which equals $\|CS\|_\infty$ to within $0.3$~L/min/K. A first-order measurement filter ($\tau_m = 0.05$~min) cuts valve travel by a factor of $1.8$ to $3.1$ at $\sigma = 0.01$~K, but the empirical rules still sit within $11\%$ of the kinematic travel ceiling at $\sigma = 0.1$~K. The ITAE-optimal tuning, designed on a $-2$~K step, remains best on out-of-sample steps of $-5$, $-1$, $+1$ and $+2$~K and ranks third at $+5$~K. Sobol indices show that tracking-error variance is governed by feed concentration and heat-transfer capacity for every tuning.
\end{abstract}
