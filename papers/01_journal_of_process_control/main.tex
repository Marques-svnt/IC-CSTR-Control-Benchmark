\begin{abstract}
In a reverse-acting PID loop whose valve is limited in amplitude and slew rate, the anti-windup logic, the discretization of its filters and the order in which the limits are evaluated are part of the controller, yet comparisons of controllers seldom report them. This study shows how these choices decide the verdict on the temperature loop of an exothermic CSTR with six fixed-gain PID loops. A proposition shows that sign-blind amplitude clamping, which reuses the direct-acting freeze condition, latches the loop at full cooling; the temperature settles $\vlatchgap$~K below the setpoint and the integral absolute error of a $-2$~K step for Ziegler--Nichols rises from $\vZNdir$ to $\vZNsign$~K$\cdot$min. An ablation over three steps, a load profile, deadband, stiction and three operating points shows that a slew-rate trigger, not sign awareness, restores the high-gain loops but worsens slow ones such as SIMC. Without measurement noise, back-calculation with one tracking constant per loop stays within $\vttcvmaxpct\%$ of the directional rule, whereas the usual defaults are up to $\vtttimaxpct\%$ worse; with $0.05$~K sensor noise it is up to $\vnzbchimaxpct\%$ worse. Placing the backlash before the slew limit and discretizing the measurement filter by explicit Euler change the IAE by up to $\vorderTwodIAE\%$ and $\vorderFourdIAE\%$ and reorder loops and policies. A heat exchanger used as a second plant confirms the trigger for the high-gain loops but shows no latch and a loop where the rate-demand heuristic fails. A pseudocode and a checklist state what a comparison must report.
\end{abstract}
