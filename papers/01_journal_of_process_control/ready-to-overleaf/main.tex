\begin{abstract}
Verdicts on PID tuning rules for exothermic reactors depend on implementation details that are rarely reported, chiefly the anti-windup logic of a valve limited in amplitude and slew rate. This implementation study uses a three-state nonlinear CSTR, eight PID tunings and a seven-policy ablation of the integrator logic. A frequent implementation error, sign-blind amplitude clamping (direct-acting conditions reused in a reverse-acting loop), latches the valve at full cooling: a proposition gives the offset equilibrium, and for Ziegler--Nichols the integral absolute error of a $-2$~K step rises from $0.940$ to $104.123$~K$\cdot$min. A slew-rate trigger restores the loop; a map over slew limits of $20$ to $320$~L/min$^2$ shows that it matters for the empirical rules at every limit and for AMIGO and the optimal tuning when the valve is slow. Back-calculation with its tracking constant inside $[T_d, T_i]$ performs as well as the directional rule, up to $34\%$ better, while the directional rule needs no per-loop constant but depends on the slew limit. Bounding $M_s \le 1.6$ on the exact linearization instead of the identified dead-time model lowers the optimal ITAE by $11\%$ in sample, with no gain out of sample, $76\%$ higher high-frequency gain and a margin that collapses under $\pm 20\%$ error of the identified model. Noise-to-valve gain follows $|K_p|(1+N)$, not the sensitivity peak. Sobol indices with bootstrap intervals, restricted to the feasible region, show that heat-removal demand explains only part of the error variance. A pseudocode and a five-item checklist support reporting.
\end{abstract}
