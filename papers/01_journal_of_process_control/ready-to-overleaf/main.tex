\begin{abstract}
Verdicts on PID tuning rules for exothermic reactors depend on implementation details that are rarely reported, among them the anti-windup logic of a valve limited in amplitude and slew rate. This implementation study fixes a three-state nonlinear CSTR with such a valve and eight PID tunings (Ziegler--Nichols, Cohen--Coon, Tyreus--Luyben, AMIGO, SIMC and an ITAE-optimal PID, with two-degree-of-freedom variants of the last two), and ablates seven integrator policies on three setpoint steps and a load profile. Sign-blind amplitude clamping, whose conditions are written for direct-acting loops, never freezes a reverse-acting integrator while it winds up: it latches the valve at full cooling, the reactor settles $16.7$~K below the setpoint and the integral absolute error of a $-2$~K step rises from $0.940$ to $104.123$~K$\cdot$min for Ziegler--Nichols. A slew-rate trigger, not sign awareness, restores the loop and needs no tuning parameter, whereas back-calculation degrades up to $38$-fold when its time constant is scaled by $0.1$ or $10$. Imposing $M_s \le 1.6$ on the exact linearization instead of the identified dead-time model lowers the optimal ITAE by $11\%$ but raises the high-frequency gain by $76\%$; optimizing the setpoint weights jointly returns the one-degree-of-freedom controller. Noise-to-valve gain is set by $|K_p|(1+N)$, not by the sensitivity peak of the exact loop ($1.57$ and $1.75$ for Ziegler--Nichols and Cohen--Coon), and a first-order measurement filter cuts valve travel by $44$ to $69\%$ at $\sigma = 0.01$~K. Sobol indices with bootstrap intervals attribute the IAE variance to feed concentration and heat-transfer capacity, and the heat-removal demand correlates with IAE (Spearman $0.43$--$0.81$).
\end{abstract}
