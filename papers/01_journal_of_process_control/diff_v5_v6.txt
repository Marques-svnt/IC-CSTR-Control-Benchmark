\subsection{Positioning against the anti-windup literature}
\label{subsec:positioning}
Anti-windup for PID loops is a mature topic with four families of solutions (Table~\ref{tab:aw_families}). Conditional integration (clamping) and back-calculation are the schemes implemented in practice \cite{astrom1989windup, hippe2006windup, peng1996nonlinear, bohn1995analysis}, and they are the two compared here. Compensators designed by linear matrix inequalities treat magnitude and rate saturation together \cite{galeani2009tutorial, tarbouriech2009antiwindup, lerch2021antiwindup}; they are not part of the comparison because they require a plant model and a design step [-that the PID practitioner studied here does not have, and-]{+outside+} the {+scope of an implementation study, whose+} question[-asked-] is what an implementation of the two industrial schemes does. Recent work refines individual schemes: rate-limited anti-windup for a fractional-order PID \cite{wang2020rate}, gradient-based optimization of anti-windup PID gains \cite{aksland2022gradient}, and learned or operator-theoretic variants of back-calculation \cite{serrano2024neuron, morohoshi2025nonlinear}. Two facts frame the present study. First, conditional integration is correctly specified by the direction of the integrator relative to the saturation (freeze when the increment pushes the command further into the stop), but it is easily implemented as the shortcut ``freeze when the command exceeds the stop and the error is positive'', which is right for a direct-acting controller only. In a reverse-acting loop ($K_p < 0$) the sign of the error that pushes the command into the stop is reversed, and a shortcut that keeps the direct-acting convention is \emph{sign-blind}. We call it \emph{sign-blind amplitude clamping} and present it as a frequent implementation error, not as a rule attributed to any publication. Second, back-calculation depends on a tracking time constant $T_t$ that is rarely [-reported together with the tuning rule it accompanies.-]{+reported.+}

[-\begin{equation}-]
[-    S_i = \frac{\mathbb{V}_{\xi_i}\!\left[\mathbb{E}(Y \mid \xi_i)\right]}{\mathbb{V}(Y)}, \qquad S_{Ti} = \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\sim i}}\!\left[\mathbb{V}(Y \mid \boldsymbol{\xi}_{\sim i})\right]}{\mathbb{V}(Y)},-]
[-    \label{eq:sobol}-]
[-\end{equation}-]
[-where $S_i$ is the share of variance explained by $\xi_i$ alone and $S_{Ti} \ge S_i$ adds its interactions. [-\paragraph{Ranges.} The box is asymmetric by design: fouling can only reduce $UA$ (to $65\%$) and the $+5\%$ allows for calibration error, whereas $E_a/R$ is known more tightly than the feed concentration.
