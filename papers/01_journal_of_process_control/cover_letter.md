# Cover letter — Journal of Process Control

**Manuscript:** Anti-Windup for Reverse-Acting PID Loops under Amplitude and Slew-Rate Limits: An Implementation Study on an Exothermic CSTR

**Authors:** Gabriel Marques de Andrade (corresponding), Elilton Rodrigues Edwards — State University of Santa Cruz (UESC), Brazil

Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript above for consideration as a research article in the *Journal of Process Control*.

**Scope.** The paper is not a ranking of PID tuning rules. It is an analysis of how the *implementation* of a PID loop (the anti-windup logic of a valve limited in amplitude and slew rate, and the measurement path) decides conclusions that the literature attributes to tuning rules. The process-control content is the mechanism: in a reverse-acting thermal loop, clamping conditions written for direct-acting loops never freeze the integrator while it winds up, latch the cooling valve at its stop, and are worse than no anti-windup. We show this on a three-state exothermic CSTR with a seven-policy ablation (three setpoint steps and a load profile), a scan of the back-calculation tracking constant, and a mechanistic account backed by the exact linearization of the plant.

**Relation to the anti-windup literature.** We do not claim a new anti-windup law. Back-calculation, conditional integration and LMI-based compensators for magnitude and rate saturation are established (Åström and Hägglund; Galeani, Tarbouriech and Turner; Tarbouriech and Turner; Hippe; Lerch et al.). Section 1.1 and Table 1 place the schemes side by side. We call the direct-acting convention reused in a reverse-acting loop "sign-blind amplitude clamping" and present it as a frequent implementation error, not as a rule attributed to any publication. What we add is (i) a controlled comparison on a reverse-acting loop with a slew-limited valve, with the tuning held fixed, on three setpoint steps, a load profile and two operating points, and a proposition that explains why the sign-blind rule latches the valve at full cooling; (ii) the finding that the slew-rate trigger, not sign awareness, restores the loop, with a map over slew limits of 20 to 320 L/min² showing when it matters, and the finding that back-calculation with its tracking constant inside [T_d, T_i] performs as well (within 3 %, up to 34 % better) at the price of a per-loop constant; (iii) the cost of imposing the sensitivity bound on the identified dead-time model instead of the exact loop (11.3 % ITAE in sample, none out of sample, 76 % more high-frequency gain and a margin that collapses under ±20 % model error), with a joint optimization of gains and setpoint weights; (iv) a noise-to-valve account based on |K_p|(1+N); and (v) a global sensitivity analysis with bootstrap intervals, conditioned on the feasible region, and a direct test of the heat-removal-margin explanation. A pseudocode and a five-item reporting checklist (Section 3.4) are intended for authors of PID comparisons.

**Relation to the companion manuscripts.** Two sibling manuscripts by the same authors are under review elsewhere and share only the plant model and the code base: *When Does Nonlinear Model Predictive Control Outperform Robust PID? A Comparative Study under Fouling and Actuator Constraints* (Computers & Chemical Engineering) compares NMPC with two of the PID tunings used here under jacket fouling, and *Thermal Controllability Limits and Cascade Control in Exothermic CSTRs under Cooling Capacity Degradation* (Chemical Engineering Research and Design) studies the heat-removal limit and cascade control. The present manuscript contains no fouling scenario, no NMPC and no cascade result; the only overlap is the nominal-step rows of the two shared tunings, which the NMPC manuscript reproduces for reference. The three are cited as companion preprints.

**Limits we state.** One reactor and one valve model; no deadband or stiction; local optimization from several starts; wide Sobol' intervals. They are listed in Section 4.11.

**Reproducibility.** All code, scripts and CSV mirrors of every table are in the public repository https://github.com/Marques-svnt/IC-CSTR-Control-Benchmark. Every number in the text is generated by the released scripts. Bibliographic data were checked against Crossref.
