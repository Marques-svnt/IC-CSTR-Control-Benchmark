\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{subsec:protocol}
All servo experiments start at $\mathbf{x}_{ss}$, apply a setpoint step at $t = 0$ (nominally $-2$~K) and last $10$~min with $\Delta t = 0.02$~min. Error metrics are computed on the \emph{true} reactor temperature, $\mathrm{IAE} = \int_0^{t_f}|T_{sp} - T|\,dt$ in K$\cdot$min. Overshoot $M_p$ (\%) is the peak excursion beyond the setpoint relative to the step size. The total variation $\mathrm{TV} = |u_0 - \bar q_j| + \sum_k |u_k - u_{k-1}|$ includes the first move away from $\bar q_j = 100$~L/min. Unless stated, the loops use the directional rule of Eq.~\eqref{eq:clamping_antiwindup}, no measurement noise and no measurement filter; all tables of this section are deterministic and carry no sampling uncertainty.

Figure~\ref{fig:classical_benchmark} and Table~\ref{tab:testbed} summarize the six loops on the nominal step. With the directional rule every loop tracks the step. \paragraph{Why sign-blind clamping locks the valve at full cooling.} For ZN and CC the sign-blind rule is worse than no anti-windup (IAE $\vZNsign$ and $\vCCsign$ against $\vZNnone$ and $\vCCnone$~K$\cdot$min), and it fails in two steps (Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}). First, the slew limit lets the command run away from the valve position (by up to $\vZNdiscrep$~L/min for ZN) while the valve is far from its stops; with $K_p < 0$ an overheated reactor ($e_k < 0$) pushes the command \emph{up}, so the sign-blind condition ``$u_{\text{raw}} \ge u_{\max}$ and $e_k > 0$'' is false and the integrator winds up. Second, the wound-up command drives the reactor below the setpoint ($e_k > 0$) while $u_{\text{raw}} \gg u_{\max}$; now the condition holds and the rule \emph{freezes} the integrator precisely when it must unwind. The valve then stays at its upper stop ($\vZNatmax$ and $\vCCatmax$~min before $t_f$) and the reactor settles at the offset equilibrium of Proposition~\ref{prop:latch}, $T(t_f) - T_{sp} = \vlatcherr$~K for both rules, independently of the tuning. With no anti-windup there is no latch, but the wound-up integrator sustains a large oscillation.

\paragraph{Factorial reading: the trigger, not the sign, restores the loop.} On the nominal step the amplitude-only variants avoid the latch but still integrate through the rate-limited moves (IAE $\vZNamp$ and $\vCCamp$~K$\cdot$min for ZN and CC), whereas the rate-aware variants recover the tracking ($\vZNrateunc$ and $\vCCrateunc$ with the unconditional freeze, $\vZNdir$ and $\vCCdir$ with the directional one). For TL, AMIGO, SIMC and Optimal ITAE the amplitude limit is never reached on this step, so the amplitude-triggered variants coincide with no anti-windup. Sign awareness is second order once the trigger is rate-aware. The conclusion does not depend on the step (Table~\ref{tab:antiwindup_scenarios}): on the $\pm 5$~K steps the amplitude-only variants leave ZN, CC, AMIGO and Optimal ITAE at IAE between $\vscenampmin$ and $\vscenampmax$~K$\cdot$min, against $\vscendirmin$ to $\vscendirmax$ with the rate-aware directional rule, so loops whose nominal step does not expose the problem are also affected by large moves. Sign-blind clamping remains worse than no anti-windup for the empirical rules on the $-5$~K step ($\vZNfivesign$ and $\vCCfivesign$ against $\vZNfivenone$ and $\vCCfivenone$) and on the load profile ($\vZNloadsign$ and $\vCCloadsign$ against $\vZNloadnone$ and $\vCCloadnone$).

\subsection{Back-calculation and its tracking constant}
\label{subsec:backcalc}
Fig.~\ref{fig:tt_curve} scans $T_t$ from $0.525\,\Delta t$ to $10\,T_i$ for the three steps. The physical range of the tracking constant is $T_d \le T_t \le T_i$; below $\Delta t$ the discrete update, which multiplies $I$ by $1 - \Delta t/T_t$, over-corrects, and below $\Delta t/2$ it diverges. A fair comparison needs one $T_t$ per loop rather than the best $T_t$ per step. The main criterion is the one a designer can apply without the directional rule: $T_t^{\mathrm{cv}}$ is chosen inside $[T_d, T_i]$ on the $-2$~K design step and judged on the $\pm 5$~K steps (Table~\ref{tab:antiwindup_tt_summary}). Its worst IAE ratio to the directional rule on $\pm 5$~K lies between $\vttcvmin$ and $\vttcvmax$ over the six loops: back-calculation with one constant chosen on the design step matches the directional rule on larger steps. A second criterion, $T_t^{\mathrm{mm}}$, minimizes the largest ratio over the three steps and gives $\vttmmmin$ to $\vttmmmax$; it is in-sample and uses the directional rule as reference, so it only confirms the first. Three of these choices are boundary solutions: for \vttcvlower{} $T_t^{\mathrm{cv}}$ sits on the lower edge, $T_t = T_d$, and for \vttcvupper{} on the upper edge, $T_t = T_i$. Without the restriction the minimum leaves the range (to $\vttfreeZN$ and $\vttfreeCC$~min, i.e.\ $\Delta t$, for ZN and CC, and to the largest value scanned, $\vttfreeSIMCovTi\,T_i$, for SIMC; Fig.~\ref{fig:tt_curve}), and the worst ratio on $\pm 5$~K does not improve (largest drop $\vttfreemaxgain$, largest rise $\vttfreemaxloss$): the physical range costs nothing out of sample, but it does not make these choices interior. For SIMC the criterion is in effect choosing little or no back-calculation: $T_t^{\mathrm{cv}} = T_i$ is the weakest tracking inside the range, it reproduces the IAE of $T_t = T_i$ exactly ($\vSIMCcvtwo$, $\vSIMCcvm$ and $\vSIMCcvp$~K$\cdot$min on $-2$, $-5$ and $+5$~K, against $\vSIMCdir$, $\vSIMCfivemdir$ and $\vSIMCfivepdir$ with the directional rule), and it stays close to no anti-windup at all ($\vSIMCnone$, $\vSIMCfivemnone$ and $\vSIMCfivepnone$). The usual defaults are worse, with worst ratios up to $\vttsqrtmax$ for $T_t = \sqrt{T_iT_d}$ and $\vtttimax$ for $T_t = T_i$, the value associated with conditioning techniques \cite{peng1996nonlinear}. The comparison is not limited to the IAE (Table~\ref{tab:antiwindup_tt_tvmp}): for ZN and CC back-calculation with $T_t^{\mathrm{cv}}$ moves the valve less in $\vempTVlower$ of $\vempcases$ cases and overshoots less in $\vempMplower$ of $\vempcases$ (CC on $+5$~K: travel $\vCCfiveTVdir$ against $\vCCfiveTVbc$~L/min, overshoot $\vCCfiveMpdir\%$ against $\vCCfiveMpbc\%$), whereas for SIMC it moves the valve $\vSIMCtvincmin$ to $\vSIMCtvincmax\%$ more. The advantage of the directional rule is therefore that it needs no per-loop constant, not that it performs better; its behaviour depends on the slew limit and on $\Delta t$ through $u_{\text{act}}$, which the next subsection maps.

\subsection{Regime map: where the slew-rate trigger matters}
\label{subsec:regime}
Fig.~\ref{fig:smax_map} and Table~\ref{tab:smax_ratio} repeat the $-2$~K step for slew limits from $20$ to $320$~L/min$^2$ with the gains fixed. For ZN and CC the amplitude-only rule is $\vmapCCmin$ to $\vmapZNmax$ times worse than the rate-aware rule at every limit, because the amplitude limit is reached and the integrator winds up during the rate-limited ramp; sign-blind clamping latches for $S_{\max} \ge \vlatchfromZN$~L/min$^2$. For AMIGO the ratio is $\vmapAMIGOtwenty$ at $20$~L/min$^2$, $\vmapAMIGOeighty$ at the benchmark value and $\vmapAMIGOlast$ at $320$, and for Optimal ITAE $\vmapOptimalITAEtwenty$, $\vmapOptimalITAEeighty$ and $\vmapOptimalITAElast$: the trigger matters when the valve is slow compared with what the loop demands. For TL the ratio stays between $\vmapTLmin$ and $\vmapTLmax$. Re-optimizing the Optimal ITAE gains at each limit gives ratios from $\vretunedfirst$ to $\vretunedlast$ (Table~\suppref{tab:smax_retuned}), so the map is not an artefact of gains designed at $80$~L/min$^2$.

\paragraph{SIMC is a counterexample.} For SIMC the rate-aware trigger makes the loop \emph{worse} than no anti-windup: the ratio is $\vSIMCratioff$, $\vSIMCratioeighty$ and $\vSIMCratiolast$ at $45$, $80$ and $320$~L/min$^2$ (it exceeds $1$ only at $20$, $\vSIMCratiotwenty$), and the IAE is $\vSIMCdir$ against $\vSIMCnone$~K$\cdot$min on the $-2$~K step, $\vSIMCfivemdir$ against $\vSIMCfivemnone$ on $-5$~K, $\vSIMCfivepdir$ against $\vSIMCfivepnone$ on $+5$~K, $\vopSIMCdir$ against $\vopSIMCnone$ at the second operating point and $\vdbSIMCdirsix$ against $\vdbSIMCnonesix$ with a $6$~L/min deadband (Section~\ref{subsec:robustness_ext}). The slow, well-damped loop asks for valve moves only slightly faster than the slew limit and only briefly ($\vTrlSIMC$~min slew-limited on the $-2$~K step, against $\vTrlothersmin$ to $\vTrlothersmax$~min for the other loops), so little windup accumulates; freezing the integrator during those samples removes integral action the loop needed, and the freeze acts as a delay rather than as a protection. To find a criterion, Table~\suppref{tab:rate_demand} relates the outcome to the rate demand $\rho = \max_k|u_k - u_{k-1}|/(S_{\max}\Delta t)$ of the same loop with the slew limit removed (setpoint kick excluded). Over $\vncases$ cases (six loops, three steps, nine limits), the trigger improved the loop in $\vnrhohihelp$ of the $\vnrhohi$ cases with $\rho \ge 1$ (worst ratio $\vrhohiratiomin$); with $\rho < 1$ the ratio ranged from $\vrholoratiomin$ to $\vrholoratiomax$, and although all $\vnrholohurt$ cases made worse are SIMC, $\rho$ does not separate SIMC from the other loops ($\vrhohelplowloop$ at $\vrhohelplowsmax$~L/min$^2$ gains $\vrhohelplowratio$ with $\rho = \vrhohelplow$). The rate-aware trigger is therefore recommended when the loop demands faster valve moves than the slew limit allows ($\rho \ge 1$); otherwise its effect must be checked for the loop at hand.

\subsection{Measurement path}
\label{subsec:measurement_path}
The noise vulnerability of the empirical rules is often attributed to a high sensitivity peak, which on the identified FOPTD is $\vMsFZN$ for ZN and $\vMsFCC$ for CC. On the exact linearization the peaks are only $\vSlinZN$ and $\vSlinCC$ (Table~\ref{tab:testbed}); the FOPTD values largely reflect its apparent delay. What separates the loops is the gain from measurement noise to the valve command, $CS = C/(1+L)$: at high frequency $CS \to C$, so its peak is the plateau of the filtered PID, $|K_p|(1+N)$, which is $\vCSZN$ and $\vCSCC$~L/min/K for ZN and CC against $\vCSSIMC$ for SIMC. A first-order measurement filter ($\tau_m = 0.05$~min) lowers the valve travel at a noise level of $\sigma = 0.01$~K by a factor of $\vfiltfactmin$ to $\vfiltfactmax$, and lowering the derivative-filter coefficient from $N = 10$ to $N = 3$ cuts $|K_p|(1+N)$ of ZN and CC by $\vNcut\%$ without changing their noise-free error (Supplementary Section~S4, $30$ noise realizations per case). The measurement path is therefore part of the implementation that a comparison must state.

\subsection{Load disturbances}
\label{subsec:load_results}
A feed temperature step $\Delta T_f = +2$~K at $t = 2$~min and a feed concentration step $\Delta C_{Af} = +10\%$ at $t = 8$~min were applied at constant setpoint (Table~\ref{tab:load}). The largest valve opening is $\vloadvalvemin$ to $\vloadvalvemax$~L/min, far from the stop, so amplitude saturation plays no part in this response. The peak deviation lies between $\vloadpeakmin$ and $\vloadpeakmax$~K for all six loops and does not discriminate between them; the IAE ranges from $\vloadCC$ (CC) to $\vloadSIMC$~K$\cdot$min (SIMC). This regulation ranking is specific to a regime in which the rate limit dominates the peak deviation; Supplementary Section~S5 repeats it with $30$ noise realizations and with the measurement filter.

\subsection{Limitations}
The study uses one reactor model with constant physical properties, a valve without stiction (deadband is simulated only for the anti-windup policies), and deterministic responses in the main text. No second process or higher-fidelity plant was tested: the transferability of the findings rests on the mechanism (loop sign and rate limit, Proposition~\ref{prop:latch}), whereas the regime map, the back-calculation constants and the loop-by-loop numbers refer to this reactor and valve. The one-sample delay of the freeze decision was not varied, and dynamic anti-windup compensators were not compared. The six test-bed loops are fixed-gain PID controllers; gain scheduling and model-based controllers are outside the scope.
