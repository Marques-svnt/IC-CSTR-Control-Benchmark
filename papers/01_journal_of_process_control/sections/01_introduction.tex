PID controllers still regulate the large majority of loops in the chemical process industries \cite{astrom2006pid, seborg2010process}, and the temperature loop of an exothermic continuous stirred tank reactor (CSTR) is one of the most demanding among them. Arrhenius kinetics couple reactor temperature and reaction rate through an exponential positive feedback, which produces steady-state multiplicity, parametric sensitivity and open-loop unstable regimes \cite{uppal1974dynamic, aris1958analysis, bequette2003process}. The cooling valve that closes the loop is bounded in stroke and in travel speed, and when an aggressive controller drives it into saturation the loop is effectively opened and the integrator winds up \cite{astrom1989windup, hippe2006windup, galeani2009tutorial, tarbouriech2009antiwindup}.

The tuning rules most often used as references for this loop are the reaction-curve rules of Ziegler and Nichols (1942) \cite{ziegler1942optimum} and Cohen and Coon (1953) \cite{cohen1953theoretical}, derived for linear, unconstrained processes \cite{odwyer2009handbook, shinskey1996process}. Their failure on exothermic reactors is a recurring finding \cite{visioli2006practical, luyben2007chemical}, and it is frequently used to motivate model-based, nonlinear or learning-based alternatives. This paper is not another ranking of tuning rules. It is an implementation study that asks how much of such verdicts is decided by details of the implementation rather than by the rule: the actuator model, the anti-windup logic and the measurement path. Anti-windup under velocity (slew-rate) limits is a known weak point of textbook schemes \cite{astrom1989windup, galeani2009tutorial, tarbouriech2009antiwindup, visioli2006practical, bohn1995analysis, peng1996nonlinear}, and a reverse-acting thermal loop ($K_p < 0$) inverts the sign convention under which the usual conditional-integration rule was written. When the logic is wrong the resulting windup is attributed to the tuning rule; when the measurement is unfiltered, the noise vulnerability is attributed to a sensitivity peak that the physical plant does not have.

The study uses a three-state CSTR model with an explicit jacket balance and a cooling valve limited to $[0, 300]$~L/min and $80$~L/min$^2$. Eight PID tunings, including modern robust baselines \cite{tyreus1992tuning, astrom2004revisiting, skogestad2003simple, grimholt2018optimal, garpinger2014performance, padula2012optimal}, are run under one protocol with error metrics evaluated on the true reactor temperature. The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A $2\times 2$ factorial ablation of clamping anti-windup (trigger: amplitude only or amplitude plus slew rate; sign awareness: unconditional or directional freeze), set against no anti-windup and back-calculation, with a mechanistic account of why the textbook rule latches a reverse-acting loop at full cooling. A directional rule that freezes the integrator whenever the amplitude \emph{or} the slew-rate limit cut the previous command and the integral increment pushes further into saturation is adopted for the benchmark.
    \item A benchmark of eight tunings---Ziegler--Nichols, Cohen--Coon, Tyreus--Luyben, AMIGO and its two-degree-of-freedom (2-DOF) variant, SIMC, and multi-start ITAE optimization with $M_s \le 1.6$ evaluated on the identified dead-time loop, with its 2-DOF variant---covering servo tracking, out-of-sample setpoint steps, unmeasured load disturbances and the robustness of the design model and of the exact linearized plant, including $\|CS\|_\infty$ and the high-frequency controller gain.
    \item A noise campaign with and without a first-order measurement filter, so that the noise-to-valve mechanism is attributed to the controller gain at high frequency rather than to a sensitivity peak.
    \item A continuous map of the trade-off between tracking error and valve travel \cite{alcantara2013simple, reynoso2021multiobjective} and a global sensitivity analysis with Sobol' indices \cite{sobol2001global, saltelli2010variance} under simultaneous uncertainty in kinetics, heat transfer, feed concentration and sensor dead time.
\end{enumerate}
