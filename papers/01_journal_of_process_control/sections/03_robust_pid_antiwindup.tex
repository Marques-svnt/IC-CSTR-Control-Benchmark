\subsection{Discrete PID with filtered derivative}
The controller is a parallel PID sampled at $\Delta t = 0.02$~min, with $u_{\text{raw},k} = u_{\text{bias}} + P_k + I_k + D_k$, $u_{\text{bias}} = q_{j,ss}$ and $e_k = y_{sp,k} - y_{pv,k}$ \cite{astrom2006pid, seborg2010process}:
\begin{align}
    P_k &= K_p e_k, \label{eq:proportional_term} \\
    I_k &= I_{k-1} + \frac{K_p}{T_i}\,\frac{e_k + e_{k-1}}{2}\,\Delta t, \label{eq:integral_trapezoidal} \\
    D_k &= \frac{\tau_f}{\tau_f + \Delta t} D_{k-1} - \frac{K_p T_d}{\tau_f + \Delta t}\,(y_{pv,k} - y_{pv,k-1}), \label{eq:derivative_filtered}
\end{align}
with derivative on the measurement and filter time $\tau_f = T_d/N$ \cite{astrom1995pid, visioli2006practical}. The coefficient $N$ is a design variable: the benchmark uses the common value $N = 10$ for every rule, and Section~\ref{subsec:noise_results} shows how much of the noise-to-valve gain of the empirical rules is due to that choice \cite{segovia2013noise}. The loop is reverse-acting: more coolant lowers the temperature, so all tunings have $K_p < 0$.

\subsection{Directional amplitude- and rate-aware clamping}
Let $u_{\text{act},k-1}$ be the valve position actually reached at the previous sample after amplitude \emph{and} slew-rate limitation. With the trapezoidal increment $\delta I_k = (K_p/T_i)\,\tfrac{1}{2}(e_k + e_{k-1})\,\Delta t$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:integral_trapezoidal}, the integrator is frozen when the previous command was cut by either limit and the increment pushes further in the same direction:
\begin{equation}
    I_k = \begin{cases}
        I_{k-1}, & \text{if } \left(u_{\text{raw},k-1} - u_{\text{act},k-1}\right)\delta I_k > 0, \\
        I_{k-1} + \delta I_k, & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:clamping_antiwindup}
\end{equation}
Evaluating the saturation residual at the previous sample avoids an algebraic loop between $I_k$, $u_{\text{raw},k}$ and the nonlinear valve map. The product form contains the sign of the loop: with $K_p < 0$ a negative error drives the command towards $u_{\max}$, so the sign-blind condition ``$u_{\text{raw}} \ge u_{\max}$ and $e_k > 0$'' is false while the integrator winds up and becomes true only when the error has reversed, i.e. when the integrator must unwind (Section~\ref{subsec:ablation}). It also covers rate saturation: a $2$~K setpoint step demands more than $S_{\max}\Delta t = 1.6$~L/min per sample from most tunings, so the valve spends the first part of the transient rate-limited but far from its amplitude stops, and an amplitude-only rule keeps integrating during the whole ramp \cite{bohn1995analysis, peng1996nonlinear}. Section~\ref{subsec:ablation} quantifies the difference against sign-blind clamping and the other variants defined next. The rule is the sign-of-increment form of conditional integration, evaluated on the slew-limited valve position; it is adopted, not proposed as new (Section~\ref{subsec:positioning}).

\subsection{Anti-windup variants of the ablation}
\label{subsec:aw_variants}
Seven integrator policies are compared (Table~\ref{tab:antiwindup_ablation}). \emph{None}: $I_k = I_{k-1} + \delta I_k$ at all times. \emph{Back-calculation} \cite{astrom1989windup, galeani2009tutorial} adds a tracking term,
\begin{equation}
    I_k = I_{k-1} + \delta I_k + \frac{\Delta t}{T_t}\left(u_{\text{act},k-1} - u_{\text{raw},k-1}\right), \qquad T_t = \sqrt{T_i T_d}\ (T_t = T_i \text{ if } T_d = 0),
    \label{eq:back_calculation}
\end{equation}
with the default $T_t$ above; because the scheme is sensitive to $T_t$, Table~\ref{tab:antiwindup_tt_sweep} repeats it with $0.1T_t$ and $10T_t$. \emph{Sign-blind amplitude clamping} freezes the integrator when $u_{\text{raw}} \ge u_{\max}$ and $e_k > 0$, or $u_{\text{raw}} \le u_{\min}$ and $e_k < 0$, which is the sign convention of a direct-acting loop. The remaining four variants form the $2\times 2$ factorial of the clamping family. The \emph{trigger} is either amplitude only ($u_{\text{raw},k-1}$ outside $[u_{\min}, u_{\max}]$) or amplitude plus slew rate ($u_{\text{raw},k-1} \ne u_{\text{act},k-1}$). The \emph{sign awareness} is either unconditional (the integrator is frozen whenever the trigger holds, whatever the sign of $\delta I_k$) or directional (frozen only if $(u_{\text{raw},k-1} - u_{\text{act},k-1})\,\delta I_k > 0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:clamping_antiwindup}). The rule adopted in the benchmark is the amplitude-plus-rate, directional cell; Algorithm~\ref{alg:integrator} gives its implementation.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Integrator update with directional amplitude- and rate-aware clamping (one sampling instant, reverse- or direct-acting).}\label{alg:integrator}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $e_k$, $e_{k-1}$, $I_{k-1}$, previous command $u_{\text{raw},k-1}$ and previous valve position $u_{\text{act},k-1}$ (after amplitude \emph{and} slew limits)
\State $\delta I \gets (K_p/T_i)\,\tfrac12 (e_k + e_{k-1})\,\Delta t$
\If{$(u_{\text{raw},k-1} - u_{\text{act},k-1})\,\delta I > 0$}
    \State $I_k \gets I_{k-1}$ \Comment{limit cut the last move and the increment pushes further into it}
\Else
    \State $I_k \gets I_{k-1} + \delta I$
\EndIf
\State $u_{\text{raw},k} \gets u_{\text{bias}} + P_k + I_k + D_k$; \quad $u_{\text{act},k} \gets \operatorname{rate}(\operatorname{sat}(u_{\text{raw},k}))$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Checklist for reporting the anti-windup logic}
\label{subsec:checklist}
A comparison of PID tunings on a valve-limited loop is only interpretable if it states the following five items; each one changed a conclusion in this study.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Loop sign.} Is the loop reverse-acting ($K_p<0$ in the convention used), and was the clamping condition written for that sign?
    \item \textbf{Trigger.} Does the logic react to the amplitude limit only, or also to the slew-rate limit (i.e.\ does it use the post-rate-limit valve position)?
    \item \textbf{Sign awareness.} Is the integrator frozen unconditionally, or only if the increment pushes further into the limit?
    \item \textbf{Tracking constant.} If back-calculation is used, what is $T_t$ and how sensitive is the result to $0.1T_t$ and $10T_t$?
    \item \textbf{Measurement path.} Which derivative-filter coefficient $N$ and which measurement filter $\tau_m$ were used, and are the errors evaluated on the true or on the measured signal?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Measurement filter}
\label{subsec:meas_filter}
Where indicated, the controller sees the reactor temperature through a first-order low-pass filter, $\tau_m\,\dot T_{\text{meas}} = T + \eta - T_{\text{meas}}$ with $\tau_m = 0.05$~min and sensor noise $\eta$ (the symbol $\tau_f$ is reserved for the derivative filter). Under the zero-order hold at the sampling instants it is integrated exactly, $T_{\text{meas},k} = T_{\text{meas},k-1} + (1 - e^{-\Delta t/\tau_m})\,(T_k + \eta_k - T_{\text{meas},k-1})$. Error metrics keep using the true temperature $T$. The filter adds lag inside the loop; the benchmark tables use $\tau_m = 0$ unless stated.

\subsection{FOPTD identification}
\label{subsec:foptd_identification}
Since $\mathbf{x}_{ss}$ is open-loop stable, a $+10$~L/min coolant step was applied to the nonlinear model and a first-order-plus-dead-time (FOPTD) model
\begin{equation}
    G_p(s) = \frac{K}{\tau s + 1}\,e^{-\theta s}
    \label{eq:foptd}
\end{equation}
was fitted by the two-point method of Sundaresan and Krishnaswamy followed by nonlinear least squares, giving $K = -0.1852$~K/(L/min), $\tau = 0.6427$~min, $\theta = 0.0935$~min (RMSE $0.0092$~K) and a lag-dominant ratio $r = \theta/\tau = 0.1455$ \cite{shinskey1996process, odwyer2009handbook}. The apparent delay lumps the two fast poles and the zero of $G_{\text{true}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:g_true_exact}; Section~\ref{subsec:margins} examines the consequence for the robustness margins.

\subsection{Tuning rules}
\label{subsec:tuning_rules}
The eight tunings span the main design philosophies for a lag-dominant loop:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ziegler--Nichols (ZN)} \cite{ziegler1942optimum}: $K_p = 1.2\tau/(K\theta)$, $T_i = 2\theta$, $T_d = 0.5\theta$.
    \item \textbf{Cohen--Coon (CC)} \cite{cohen1953theoretical}: $K_p = \tau/(K\theta)\,(4/3 + r/4)$, $T_i = \theta(32 + 6r)/(13 + 8r)$, $T_d = 4\theta/(11 + 2r)$.
    \item \textbf{Tyreus--Luyben (TL)} \cite{tyreus1992tuning, luyben2007chemical}: the ultimate frequency solves $\theta\omega_u + \arctan(\tau\omega_u) = \pi$ exactly (Brent's method), $P_u = 2\pi/\omega_u$, $K_u = \operatorname{sign}(K)\sqrt{1 + (\tau\omega_u)^2}/|K|$, and $K_p = K_u/2.2$, $T_i = 2.2P_u$, $T_d = P_u/6.3$. Here $\omega_u = 17.74$~rad/min and $K_u = -61.81$. The rule was calibrated for PI control; its PID form yields $M_s = 2.32$ on this loop.
    \item \textbf{AMIGO} \cite{astrom2004revisiting, astrom2006pid}: $K_p = (0.2 + 0.45\,\tau/\theta)/K$, $T_i = \theta\,(0.4\theta + 0.8\tau)/(\theta + 0.1\tau)$, $T_d = 0.5\theta\tau/(0.3\theta + \tau)$, designed for $M_s \approx 1.4$ by maximizing the integral gain.
    \item \textbf{2-DOF AMIGO}: the AMIGO gains with setpoint weights $\beta = 0.5$, $\gamma = 0$ (Section~\ref{subsec:two_dof}), the use for which AMIGO is intended in servo tasks \cite{astrom2004revisiting}.
    \item \textbf{SIMC} \cite{skogestad2003simple, grimholt2018optimal}: $K_p = \tau/(K(\tau_c + \theta))$, $T_i = \min(\tau, 4(\tau_c + \theta))$, $T_d = 0.33\theta$, with $\tau_c = 0.40$~min.
    \item \textbf{Optimal ITAE ($M_s^{\mathrm{FOPTD}} \le 1.6$)}: Nelder--Mead minimization of the ITAE of a $-2$~K step on the nonlinear plant and the benchmark valve, with the penalty $10^4\max(0, M_s - 1.6)^2$ and $M_s$ evaluated on the identified dead-time loop of Eq.~\eqref{eq:foptd}; the superscript records that the bound is imposed on the design model, not on the exact loop (Section~\ref{subsec:constraint_cost}). Evaluating $M_s$ on the delay-free Jacobian instead would return values close to one for almost any gain and silently disable the constraint. Because the simplex is local, it is started from a default guess and from every analytical tuning already inside the robust set (AMIGO, SIMC). The default start stops at a PI-like local optimum ($T_d = 0$, $\mathrm{ITAE} = 0.34$~K$\cdot$min$^2$); the analytical starts reach $0.20$--$0.21$~K$\cdot$min$^2$. The retained optimum has $M_s^{\mathrm{FOPTD}} = 1.60$: the constraint is active on the design model, and the derivative action is kept because it buys the phase margin that allows a higher proportional gain at the same $M_s$.
    \item \textbf{2-DOF Optimal ITAE}: the optimal gains with the setpoint weights fixed at $\beta = 0.5$, $\gamma = 0$. It is a 2-DOF \emph{variant}, not an optimized 2-DOF design; the joint optimization of gains and weights is reported in Section~\ref{subsec:constraint_cost}.
\end{enumerate}
The resulting gains are listed in Table~\ref{tab:classical_benchmark}.

\subsection{Two-degree-of-freedom setpoint weighting}
\label{subsec:two_dof}
A 2-DOF structure weights the reference in the proportional and derivative paths \cite{astrom2006pid}. To avoid a bias from absolute temperatures when $\beta < 1$, the weights act on deviations from $y_{ss} = 396.65$~K:
\begin{equation}
    e_p = \beta\,\Delta y_{sp} - \Delta y_{pv}, \qquad e_i = y_{sp} - y_{pv}, \qquad e_d = \gamma\,\Delta y_{sp} - \Delta y_{pv},
    \label{eq:two_dof}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta y = y - y_{ss}$, so that $P_k = K_p e_{p,k}$, the integrator is driven by $e_{i,k}$ and the derivative by $e_{d,k}$; $\gamma = 0$ recovers derivative on the measurement. Because $\beta$ and $\gamma$ act only on the reference path, the loop transfer function $L(s) = G_p(s)C(s)$, and therefore $M_s$ and the load response, are those of the 1-DOF controller with the same gains.

\subsection{Sensitivity peak and margins}
\label{subsec:sensitivity_theory}
Robustness is quantified by the sensitivity peak \cite{astrom2006pid, skogestad2005multivariable}
\begin{equation}
    M_s = \sup_{\omega \ge 0} \left|\frac{1}{1 + G_p(j\omega)C(j\omega)}\right|,
    \label{eq:sensitivity_func}
\end{equation}
evaluated with the exact delay $e^{-j\omega\theta}$ and $C(s) = K_p\,(1 + 1/(T_i s) + T_d s/(\tau_f s + 1))$. The bound $M_s \le 1.6$ guarantees a gain margin of at least $2.67$ and a phase margin of at least $36.4^\circ$ \cite{garpinger2014performance, padula2011tuning}. The FOPTD and the true plant agree at low frequency but diverge above the identification bandwidth: the delay adds phase lag without bound, whereas the phase of the relative-degree-two $G_{\text{true}}$ saturates. The FOPTD therefore overestimates the high-frequency lag, which makes $M_s$ conservative for loops whose peak lies at high frequency, while near crossover the comparison can go either way. Section~\ref{subsec:margins} evaluates both loops.
