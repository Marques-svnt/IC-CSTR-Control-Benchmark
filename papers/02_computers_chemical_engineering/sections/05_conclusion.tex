% ============================================================
%  Section 5: Conclusions
% ============================================================

This paper compared a slew-rate-constrained NMPC, with true-state access (Oracle) and with an augmented EKF/moving-horizon estimator (NMPC-MHE), against the two strongest robust PID tunings of an exothermic CSTR, AMIGO and Optimal ITAE with $M_s \le 1.6$, all acting through the same saturating, rate-limited valve with directional anti-windup on the PID side. Five criteria delimit when the computational overhead of nonlinear control pays off on this reactor:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Near the design point, an $M_s$-constrained PID is sufficient.} The NMPC improves the nominal servo IAE of Optimal ITAE by $2.3\%$ and the wide-range tracking IAE of AMIGO by $2.5\%$. The relevant baseline for a nonlinear controller is a robustly tuned PID with correct anti-windup.
    \item \textbf{Nonlinear prediction pays off after a change of the plant that the model represents.} After an abrupt loss of heat transfer to $70\%\,UA$, NMPC-Oracle reduces the IAE of unfiltered AMIGO by $21.0\%$ and that of Optimal ITAE by $25.2\%$; against AMIGO with a measurement filter the reduction of NMPC-MHE is $13.5\%$, and NMPC-MHE stays within $1.7\%$ of the Oracle. The ladder of Section~\ref{subsec:deconf} attributes $24.6\%$ of this gain to online adaptation of $\hat\alpha_{UA}$ (reproduced by a gain-scheduled PID), none to nonlinear prediction and $76.8\%$ to slew-rate-aware planning. NMPC-MHE reduces valve travel by $15$--$24\%$ against unfiltered PID loops; against a filtered PID it uses more.
    \item \textbf{Without a disturbance model, nonlinear prediction does not help regulation; with one, it does.} Under unmeasured feed shocks both original NMPC variants settle with an offset of $+8.69$ and $+8.91$~K, while both PID loops return to the setpoint. The offset does not depend on the horizon ($N = 30$--$100$): the prediction reaches $T_{sp}$ while the plant does not. With integrating disturbance states the offset falls to $0.00$ and $-0.02$~K and the IAE to $12.60$ and $12.84$~K$\cdot$min, $15.9$--$17.5\%$ below AMIGO. Under $\pm 10\%$ model mismatch the original NMPC keeps a mean offset of $3.6$~K, the offset-free one $\le 0.003$~K.
    \item \textbf{Output feedback removes part of the advantage.} The Oracle is invariant to sensor noise; NMPC-MHE, the implementable variant, uses valve travel comparable to that of a PID loop once $\sigma \ge 0.1$~K, although it still converts less noise into thermal deviation ($\mathrm{IAE} = 3.33$ versus $4.16$~K$\cdot$min at $\sigma = 1.0$~K).
    \item \textbf{No controller crosses the thermodynamic boundary.} At $T_{sp} = 394.65$~K the energy balance with full cooling is solvable only for $UA \ge UA_{\mathrm{crit}} = 63.75\%\,UA_{\mathrm{nom}}$; at $60\%$ the only equilibrium is $396.65$~K $> T_{sp}$. The scenario is a demonstration of physical loss of controllability, not a controller ranking, and the equal errors ($30.56$--$31.08$~K$\cdot$min) reflect the heat-removal capacity of the jacket, not the control law.
\end{enumerate}
The cost of these benefits is moderate: $31.46$ and $39.28$~ms per sample for NMPC-Oracle and NMPC-MHE against $0.09$~ms for a PID loop, well within the $1.2$~s sampling interval. The practical recommendation is therefore to adopt NMPC on such reactors when operation is dominated by changes of the plant, such as fouling or wide operating ranges, and to retain a robust PID, or to add an integrating disturbance model to the NMPC, when operation is dominated by unmeasured feed disturbances. Future work will repeat the multi-point test with filtered PID loops, separate $d_T$ from $\hat\alpha_{UA}$ during fouling transients, and test the criteria on reactors with structural model mismatch.
